# Copy

Loaded at step 7 (Voice & Content section) and whenever the direction includes headline or CTA wording. The build writes the page copy from these rules.

## 1. Facts first

- Write copy from a fact sheet built at intake, before layout.
- Never invent: metrics, prices, hours, years in business, counts ("500+ families"), testimonials, customers, awards, certifications, staff names, team size. Unknown = a clearly labelled placeholder (`data-assumed`, visible bracket text like "[opening hours to confirm]") and a line under Known gaps.
- Reviews only as real quotes with name or initials, date and source. If there are none, there is no review section.
- No lorem ipsum, no "Feature one". Placeholder copy reads like real content and is marked.

## 2. Voice

- 2-3 tone words per client, each with what it rules out. Example: "plain (no euphemism stacks), steady (no urgency), local (street names, not 'our community')".
- Canonical terms with banned synonyms (e.g. "funeral" not "celebration"; "quote" not "estimate" if the shop says quote). One name per action and concept throughout.
- Plain words from the customer's view, active voice, second person. Describe, don't sell.
- Local voice: the town, street, landmark and district names customers use. Use the client's own words from their site, signage and reviews.

## 3. Headings

- H1 names the service and the place or buyer ("Funerals and cremations in <Town> since <verified year>" or "Laser cutting and welding for <region> manufacturers").
- Every heading contains a noun. No two-fragment headings ("Crafted. Trusted.").
- Sentence case by default.

## 4. Labels (buttons and links)

- Verb + object, naming the result: "Call us now", "See today's services", "Request a quote", "Send your drawings", "Plan a funeral in advance".
- Never: Submit, Send (alone), OK, Continue, Get started, Learn more, Read more, Click here, Discover.
- The main action is worded identically in nav, hero, closing section and the mobile bar.
- No "→" glyph on buttons unless it is the site's one named hover idea.
- In-progress labels end with "…" ("Sending…").

## 5. Banned vocabulary (lint)

delve, elevate, unlock, seamless, unleash, cutting-edge, robust, bespoke, streamline, empower, world-class, game-changer, next-gen, tapestry, nestled, journey (as metaphor), elevate your, solutions (as a noun on its own), industry-leading, state-of-the-art, passionate about, second to none, look no further, in today's fast-paced world, we pride ourselves.

## 6. Banned cadences

- "X. No Y." runs ("Real steel. No shortcuts.").
- "Not X, but Y" / "It's not just X, it's Y".
- Two-fragment and three-fragment headings ("Honest. Local. Family.").
- "WORD — fragment" labels.
- Em-dash saturation: more than one em dash per paragraph. Em dashes for real breaks are fine in moderation.
- Rhetorical question openers ("Looking for…?").
- Rule-of-three adjective lists on every line.

Over-corrections are also tells: comma splices replacing dashes, calm virtue words ("thoughtful", "considered", "intentional") replacing hype, everything in pairs instead of threes.

## 7. Punctuation and numbers

- At most one exclamation mark per page; none in success or error messages; none at all on funeral sites.
- Numerals for counts; non-breaking space between number and unit; en dash in ranges; local date, time and currency format.
- Ampersands only in names and logos.
- Curly quotes and real apostrophes.
- Mark brand names `translate="no"` (build).

## 8. Messages

- Errors: what went wrong and how to fix it. No "Oops", no apology.
- Success and thank-you: what happens next and when ("We'll call you back within one working day" only if the client confirms that promise; otherwise a neutral "We'll reply by phone or email").
- Forms: one plain consent sentence under the submit button with a privacy link.

## 9. Niche notes

- Funeral: no euphemism stacks ("passed on to their heavenly journey"), "celebration of life" not as a slogan, no urgency or discount language, no condolence clichés as headings, no emoji.
- Metal fabrication: facts over adjectives (materials, max sheet size, tolerances, lead times only if the client gives them), no "precision engineered excellence", no invented capacity figures.
